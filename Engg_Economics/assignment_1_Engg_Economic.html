Q1

What is Managerial Economics?

Answer: Managerial Economics is the application of economic theories and principles to solve business problems and make better managerial decisions. Example: A manager uses demand and cost information to decide the price of a product.

Q2

What is the relationship of Managerial Economics with other disciplines?

Answer: Managerial Economics is related to the following disciplines:

(i)Economics: Provides theories of demand, supply, cost and pricing.

(ii) Accounting: Provides financial information for decision-making.

(iii)Statistics: Helps in forecasting and analysing data.

(iv) Mathematics: Helps in calculations and optimization.

(v)Finance: Helps in investment and financial decisions.

(vi)Marketing: Helps in understanding customers, demand and pricing.

(vii)In short:Managerial Economics uses knowledge from different subjects to make better business decisions.

Q3

What is a Firm? What are the main types of Firms? What are the main objectives and goals of a Firm?

Answer: A firm is an organization that produces goods or services to satisfy customer needs and earn returns.

The main types of firms are:

Sole Proprietorship: Owned and controlled by one person.

Partnership:Owned by two or more persons.

Company:A separate legal business entity owned by shareholders.

Co-operative Society:Formed by people with a common interest.

Note: Profit maximization is traditionally considered one of the major objectives of a firm.
The main objectives of a firm are:

  • Profit maximization
  • Sales maximization
  • Growth of the business
  • Customer satisfaction
  • Increasing market share
  • Survival in the market
  • Employee welfare

Q4

What is a Managerial Decision?What are the steps in Managerial Decision-making?

Answer: A managerial decision is a choice made by a manager from different available alternatives to achieve the objectives of an organization.
Example: A manager decides whether to increase production or maintain the current production level.

The steps in Managerial Decision-making:

  • Identify the problem.
  • Collect information.
  • Identify alternatives.
  • Evaluate alternatives.
  • Select the best alternative.
  • Implement the decision.
  • Review the result.
